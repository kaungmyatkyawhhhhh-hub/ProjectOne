# Watch study: organic Roblox marketing (TikTok)

- **Why:** the 16 Nov Training inbox flagged an "ungatekeeping the organic Roblox marketing method" clip (about 6.4K likes) that was only seen up to the intro.
- **How it was watched:** in the built-in browser with `tiktok_watch_in_browser.js`. That gave every frame, the sound measured (not heard), the captions, and the comments with the creator's replies. Nothing was liked or followed.
- **Finding the clip:**
  - Five TikTok searches found no clip with that exact title and about 6.4K likes.
  - The closest match is the robuncolous clip below. TikTok's own page title for it is "Roblox Organic Marketing: How 2 Games Blew Up for Free".
  - A second clip (Isaac) is the one that actually explains an organic method, so both are here.
  - If the owner remembers a different clip, a link is enough to watch it.

## 1. robuncolous: "best part is it's FREE!"
- **Link:** tiktok.com/@robuncolous/video/7695821502939270413
- **Posted:** 12 Oct 2026, by a creator with 3,550 followers.
- **Stats:** 47.6K views, 4,106 likes, 1,815 saves, 337 shares, 96 comments.
- **Format:**
  - 13.8 s, a single talking-head shot with no cuts and no speech; the sound is a trending track (about 102 BPM).
  - One caption stays on screen the whole time: "Organic Marketing is still highly underutilized by game developers and probably is the best way to build a loyal audience."
  - Overlays:
    - A stock "Organic Marketing Strategies" mind map: evergreen content, SEO, engaging value-driven content, white-hat links, mobile-optimised content, social media.
    - Two game cards labelled "(2 games that blew up primarily through organic marketing)":
      - **Build and Conquer [V0.1]** (The Slop Company): 86% likes, 11.2K playing;
      - **[COSMETICS] Command An Army** (Fight, Fight, Fight!): 95% likes, 20K playing.
- **The method itself:** there isn't one. The clip is a claim plus two examples; the mind map is a generic web-marketing graphic (SEO, backlinks), not Roblox steps. It works as a TikTok because it's a bold claim over 2 proof screenshots, with a loop length (14 s) and a high save rate (44% of likes).
- **What the comments add (more useful than the clip):**
  - "Caa got famous from that 1 TikTok video": Command An Army reportedly took off from a single viral TikTok.
  - Someone says they are "the Marketing Director for Command an Army", so it had a marketing person, not just luck.
  - The creator's replies:
    - "still think organic marketing is good though especially to retain a fan base", answering "it's all staged, games buy ads";
    - "atp you don't even need organic marketing", to a dev showing a big game.
  - Another commenter: "organic advertising … 10/10 but only IF your game is good".
  - Kids judge thumbnails and UI hard:
    - "zoom out the thumbnail 20x and squint: if you can't tell what's happening within a second, it's a bad thumbnail";
    - "I'm scrolling past it just because of the AI artwork";
    - "if they couldn't be bothered to make the UI … the entire game is AI".

    AI-looking thumbnails and UI get called "slop" on sight, and Build and Conquer is called "ai slop" for its UI.
  - Many replies are devs begging for help or players for a game with no plan: posting your game in comments doesn't work.

## 2. Isaac (isaacrocreate): "Marketing is super simple when you put in the work"
- **Link:** tiktok.com/@isaacrocreate/video/7706359489431178516
- **Posted:** about 11 Nov 2026. 18.4K views, 1,334 likes, 50 s, voice with captions.
- **His three tips** (transcript, in short):
  1. If your game is a proven concept (e.g. a "Steal a X" game), plays ads alone may reach the 250 limit. A new concept may also need engagement ads.
  2. With good social media marketing you may not need Roblox ads at all: "make your budget $0 and still have a successful game".
  3. "The sauce": scroll TikTok and Instagram, find every creator who posts **game recommendations**, message them, **ask for their analytics**, offer money, and they post your game. "You're gonna see the players start rolling in."

  Same idea as the 16 Nov inbox line "paying someone with a large following 20-30 GBP can beat ads". So this is really **paid creator posts**, which is cheaper than ads but not free.
- The same creator posted "Experience pages are slept on big time" and "15k robux at 13 CCU".

## What this means for us
1. **Organic = post our own clips + get game-rec creators to post us.** Neither works if the game page or thumbnail looks AI-made (the comments are brutal about it). Order: game page first ([[K05 Marketing, ads and storefront]], skill roblox-icon-thumbnail), then clips.
2. **One viral clip can make a game** (Command An Army). Our clip pipeline (skill roblox-game-video-pipeline) should aim for the formats that worked:
   - a bold claim + 2 proof screenshots, about 14 s;
   - satisfying clips of the game's best moment;
   - unfinished VFX process clips (see [[K06 Art, UI, VFX, sound and lighting]]).
3. **Game-rec creators:** check their analytics (views, audience age) before paying; prefer creators whose audience matches the game. Paying a creator is spending money, so it's always the owner's decision.
4. **Organic also keeps the fan base**, not just new players: regular dev-log clips.
5. **Not confirmed:** whether these two games really grew "primarily" through organic marketing. One commenter says big jumps are usually paid ads.
