## What makes these shirts look "expensive" (the design lessons)

1. **Baked-in shading.** Every shirt has painted folds, wrinkles and soft shadows (satin creases on the bomber, puffy quilt panels on the vest). A flat-color shirt looks cheap; shaded fabric reads as 3D on a blocky R6 body.
2. **Limited palette.** Each outfit is one main color plus black and silver: red/black/silver, all black, white/black, black/grey. No rainbow colors.
3. **Small "hardware" details.** Zippers, studs along the sleeves, belts with buckles, chains, cross pendants, tiny patches and tags. They catch the light and make it look detailed.
4. **Graphics on the sleeves and back, not just the front.** Big script lettering runs down the sleeve, and tattoo line art sits on the arms. The sides and back matter because the turntable shows them.
5. **Layered look in 2D.** An open zip hoodie painted over a graphic tee, or a vest over a shirt. One flat texture fakes two layers.
6. **Styles that work:** streetwear / opium / grunge / "emo y2k": bombers, puffers, studded black, graphic tees, polos with chains.
7. **Warning:** the red bomber has a real fashion-brand wordmark on the sleeve. Do not copy brand names or logos. Roblox moderation removes them and can strike the account. Use our own made-up wordmarks instead.

## How to use this for our shirts

- Design a **small drop of 4–5 shirts** in one shared style (for example a dark streetwear set), each with shading, hardware details and sleeve graphics.
- Sell them cheap (5–10 Robux each) from a group, then make our own "CHEAP OUTFITS" TikTok in this format: dark studio, turntable, CODE text, group card at 2 s.
- The turntable studio look can be built in Roblox Studio (dark backdrop, reflective floor, one key light) and recorded with the roblox-game-video-pipeline skill.
