## What the five share
1. **A claim or a doubt in the first second**: "This took Claude just 30 minutes", "claude isn't good at making models", "this video went viral ... wrong question", a version number counting up. Not a logo sting alone (only #1 opens on a logo, and it is drawing on by 0.25 s over a bright moving light).
2. **Proof, shown not told**: the product being used (#1), real chart (#3), real models on a real screen (#5), the piece playing on a real monitor (#2, #4).
3. **The screen inside the real world** in 3 of 5: a monitor in a dark room, a laptop on a desk, a phone filming a screen, with a lamp that matches the brand colour and one plain caption. It reads as "a person made this", not "an ad".
4. **Short**: four of five are 10.8-15.7 s. The long one is a voice-over tutorial.
5. **A trending original sound**, not custom music, in all five. When the sound has a beat, cuts sit within 0-60 ms of a hit.
6. **A reason to comment**: "comment 'prompt' / 'skill'" (493 and 1,454 comments), or a question as the caption (#3).
7. Polish is NOT what ranks them: the rawest one (#5) has the most views; the most polished (#1) is fourth. Polish earns saves and client work; the idea earns the views.

### Type, colour, blur, opacity, VFX, SFX: the detail pass (owner: "mind the text fonts ... and every detail of it")
Sources: nemovideo.com/blog/best-capcut-fonts-short-form, clipspeed.ai/blog/best-fonts-for-captions.html (rounded sans for playful brands; Anton / Archivo Black only for one shouted word; bold or heavier; about a tenth of the frame height; a dark outline, shadow or plate behind white text; ONE accent colour on the keyword; one caption style for the whole video; many popular fonts are personal-use only, so check the licence), github.com/khzaw/awesome-myanmar-unicode (list of Burmese fonts).
- **Burmese display fonts tested side by side** (13, rendered on a real frame): heavy enough for video = Myanmar Kuttar (wide, chunky, rounded), Myanmar Khyay (wide, squarish), Myanmar Yinmar (slab-like), Myanmar Thuriya (condensed, the "Anton" of Burmese), Noto Sans Myanmar Black (clean, neutral). Too thin for captions: Sabae, Pauklay, Gantgaw, Sanpya, Tagu, Padauk Bold. The khmertype fonts are on GitHub (github.com/khmertype/<name>) under the SIL Open Font License = free for commercial video.
- **Pairing chosen for FlexiToys**: display = **Titan One** (Latin) + **Myanmar Kuttar** (Burmese): both chunky and rounded, like the shop's own logo. Small UI lines (product name, address pill, TikTok handle) = Fredoka + Noto Sans Myanmar. The wordmark "FlexiToys" stays in Fredoka (closest to the real logo). Rejected: Thuriya + Lilita One (condensed, harder to read at speed).
- **Style chosen (3 tested)**: gradient fill (yellow #fff6b0 -> #ffd23a -> #ff9a14 for the accent word, white -> #d9cfff for the rest) + purple outline (0.11 em, #2a1262 from the shop's sign) + a short solid purple extrusion (0.09 em) + a soft shadow. Rejected: flat fill with outline (plain) and a white keyline "sticker" (lumpy around Burmese stacked letters).
- Kuttar and Titan One are WIDE: sizes came down about 8-15% (hook 116 px, captions 100-112 px, price 158 px) and a 2-line maximum was re-checked on stills.
- **Blur**: the whole ad is rendered at 120 fps and 4 sub-frames are blended per output frame, so text pops, whips, punches and rings carry real motion blur. The end-card footage is blurred 7 px as well as tinted (depth, crisp text). The shot blurs 5 px on the stop-down.
- **Opacity**: top caption scrim 50% -> 0 over the top third; end-card tint 78 / 40 / 72%; echo copies 55% -> 0; colour ghosts 45%.
- **SFX**: the effects-only file has NO pitched sounds (no bells, no tonal pops, a noise riser instead of a tone), because it sits under a song whose key is unknown; peak -6 dBFS. The own-music file keeps the pitched pops (in key with our own track). Whooshes peak 40-100 ms before the cut so the hit on the cut still reads.

| zensu.visuals (1) | prompt typed with a cursor | the TikTok handle types out with a cursor and key clicks |
| zensu.visuals (1) | glow and light on the mark | a light sweep travels across the keyword after it lands; a short glow on the hit |
- **Burmese must be animated per SYLLABLE, never per character.** `Intl.Segmenter` (grapheme) splits the AA vowel (U+102C) off its consonant and the lone mark renders with a dotted circle. Rule used: a consonant starts a new unit unless it is followed by asat (U+103A), dot-below + asat, or virama (U+1039); marks join the unit before them. Checked on every word in the ad.
- A slam from 3.4x scale with a springy overshoot dips to about 0.57x for a few frames (looks like a glitch): clamp the overshoot on big scale ranges.
- Reserve the space of words that have not landed yet (render them invisible), or the line re-centres and the earlier words jump.

## 11 Nov, later: reference study - "dolce & gabbana trend" collage edit (the look the owner picked)
Link: tiktok.com/@lorcity89/video/7641134403615214865 - 4.1M views, 614.6K likes, 112K saves, 35.6K followers, posted 2026-05-18, 4:3 (1440x1080), 11.56 s. Sound: "the kill 2" - Lex Amarni & 2muchmotion.
**Measured grid:** beat 0.5825 s (about 103 BPM), first hit 0.086 s, DROP on beat 8 = 4.746 s. A scene change on every beat after the drop, a big whip every 2 beats. Level: loud 0-1.5 s, dip 2.0-4.5 s, drop 4.5 s.
**Structure:** product cut-out + small caption on pale paper (0.1 s) -> giant wordmark scene (0.67 s) -> three more cut-outs, a quarter-second each -> wordmark again -> type scene (one bold line, underline, two small squares, a second coloured line) -> colour panels with big stacked letters slide over it -> DROP -> one collage scene per beat (diagonal ribbon, poster, four cards, emblem), repeated with new products -> paper whip -> dark end card with flares.
**Style guide (grammar only; never its logos, names or lyrics):**
- Paper: very pale pink, soft vignette, fine grain. Everything in the intro is ink on paper.
- Wordmark: heavy geometric sans (Futura Bold family), near-black, soft dark halo, a thin light edge top-left (embossed look), a light sweep across it. Under it one line of spaced serif caps with glitch slices and hairlines. Vertical side text. Small cut-outs around it.
- Ink: scratchy zigzag scribbles in the corners (a firm line plus two hair lines), long tapered brush blades, some of them out of focus.
- Cut-outs: soft dark shadow in the intro; thick WHITE sticker outline + white glow after the drop.
- Type scene: bold wide grotesque caps with a heavy soft shadow underneath, a coloured underline under one word, two small rounded squares above the first letters, the second line smaller, coloured, slab serif, right-aligned.
- Panels: two tall colour panels slide in (one from the side, one from above); marker-pen letters stacked on the first, a thin italic serif with line doodles on the second.
- Drop scenes: two-colour gradient ground; a diagonal ribbon with the brand name repeated huge, dark and slightly out of focus, sliding along the ribbon; hazard stripe on the bottom edge; streaks of light travelling along curves with a glow; four-point sparkles; out-of-focus blossoms in two opposite corners in the foreground; poster panel with spaced Didone caps, a star watermark and the product in the middle; four "postage stamp" cards (perforated coloured edge) tilted in 3D around a turning cut-out over a blurred showroom; everything drifts in 3D (slow rotateY + push-in) for the whole beat.
- Finish: highlight bloom, extra saturation, light sharpening, grain; one two-frame negative flash; white flash + shake on the drop.

- Fonts (all free licences): League Spartan 900 (wordmark), Abril Fatface (glitch serif line, ribbon), Montserrat 800/900 (labels), Alfa Slab One (second line, prices), Permanent Marker (panel letters), Cormorant Garamond italic (panel price), Bodoni Moda 900 (poster title), Noto Sans Myanmar 900 + Noto Serif Myanmar 900 (Burmese). Latin punctuation inside a Burmese line needs a Latin font in the family list, or it falls back to a default serif.
- Cut-outs: a flat box front photographed straight on -> 4 corner points -> perspective-rectify -> rounded-corner alpha. A 3D box -> polygon mask by hand. Loose objects on a table -> rembg (u2net) on a tight crop. Sticker outline = dilate the alpha 17 px per 1000 px, white underneath. Sharp phone PHOTOS beat video frames by a wide margin: ask for photos of the hero products.
- 3D: CSS `perspective` + `preserve-3d`; a layer pushed back with translateZ must not sit behind an un-pushed background plane (the ribbon vanished until the background got a larger negative Z).
- Transitions: whip with the fastest frame ON the beat (incoming scene covers the outgoing one, both travel the same way), zoom-punch for the paper cuts, spin for two of them; real motion blur from the 120 fps blend.
- TikTok safe area: keep words above y = 1600 (the app's caption bar) and away from the right-hand buttons.
- Motion blur on whips: blending 4 sub-frames is NOT enough when a layer travels over 100 px per sub-frame; the frame shows 4 ghost copies. Fix: each moving layer also gets a directional blur sized from its own speed (SVG `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="sx sy"`, sx/sy = half the distance travelled per sub-frame, plus a small all-direction amount for zoom and spin). Result: clean streaks, like a real whip.
- Finish pass in ffmpeg after the blend: bloom from highlights only (`curves` cut below 0.78 -> `gblur=24` -> `screen` at 0.6), +12% saturation, light unsharp, grain. A bloom of the whole picture washes pale paper to white.
- Extra "shine": a diagonal band of light sweeping across the wordmark, the stamp cards and the posters, 0.1-0.5 s after each lands; a warm light leak in two opposite corners (screen blend).
- Render time on this box: 1512 frames at 120 fps took about 26 min; the finish pass about 3 min.
