# Carve MEGA BITE's sharks from flat facets

MEGA BITE's sharks will reach Fisch quality by **switching style, not by polishing the current one**. Fisch's sharks are not smooth, subdivided "Pixar" models with painted textures. Our own look at in-game Fisch screenshots (9 Nov 2026) shows **low-poly, flat-shaded faceted models with hard edges, 2–4 flat colour regions, a glowing emissive eye and a wide-open mouth lined with white triangle teeth**. Roblox's own spotlight on Fisch confirms the build method behind that look: a solo developer made the fish from separately coloured parts, and nearly all variety comes from recolours and effects ([DevForum Creator Spotlight](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creator-spotlight-woozynate-makes-a-splash-with-fisch/3269481)). Our Reef Shark v2 fails for the opposite reasons. It has a round, inflated, smoothly shaded body, a thin closed grin with a picket fence of tiny teeth, a black bead eye with no glow, and painted-on gill lines, so it looks like a generic 3D toy rather than a native Roblox creature. The fix is a code-driven Blender generator that builds a deliberate low-poly cage (about 1.5–3K triangles), shapes it with hard planes (wedge head, brow ridge, jaw line, keel), assigns colour per face region, and gives every shark an emissive eye and a big open toothy mouth. A render-and-measure quality gate then blocks any model that fails the silhouette, facet, palette or 128 px read tests. The style also suits phones: flat colours avoid the texture-streaming blur that has hit SurfaceAppearance on mobile in 2026, and they read clearly without shadows on low-end devices. The plan below rebuilds the Reef Shark first as the template, then derives the other six evolutions from the same generator.

## Fisch wins with facets, flat colour and a glowing eye

The direct visual check is the most important finding, because the text sources could not describe Fisch's models at all (the wiki and guide sites with images were blocked). In a gameplay frame of the Megalodon, **every face is a visible flat facet with hard edges everywhere**. The head is a wedge with a flat top plane and an angular brow ridge that hoods the eye, and the snout is a blunt faceted cone. The eye is **a cyan emissive point, the brightest spot on the model**. The mouth is wide open, with a dark maroon interior and a zig-zag of white triangular teeth on both the upper and lower jaw that reaches back under the eye. The colour is just two flat tones, deep navy on top and white below. The boundary between them is **a jagged line that follows the low-poly edges** from the mouth corner to the tail. Gill slits are dark cut-in lines, and the fins are thin angular plates: a tall triangular dorsal, long pectorals, and a crescent tail with a bigger upper lobe. Fisch's Great White uses the same recipe: faceted, grey over white in flat colours, a long slender body, a tall dorsal fin and no texture detail. These observations come from a handful of thumbnails and gameplay frames, and thumbnails can be edited, so polygon counts and exact material settings are still unverified.

The primary source explains why this look is achievable for us. Fisch was **scripted, modelled and built by one developer, WoozyNate, in about four months**. Each fish's weight seeds a procedural pass that changes **"the attributes of each part separately"**, so hue and tint vary and every fish has **about 25 mutations**. A colour-adjust plugin did much of the recolouring ([DevForum Creator Spotlight](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creator-spotlight-woozynate-makes-a-splash-with-fisch/3269481)). The mutation list shows where the visual excitement lives. It is almost entirely whole-model recolours (Albino, Midas gold, Nuclear green), material swaps (Translucent, Glossy, Negative), encasings (Amber, Frozen) and particle auras (Electric sparks, Scorched flames), not new meshes ([Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/roblox/news/all-mutations-in-fisch-and-what-they-do)). The Megalodon itself has species variants such as Ancient and Phantom ([rblxguide](https://rblxguide.com/games/fisch/fish/megalodon)), and in screenshots the Phantom is a translucent neon version of the same model. The quality bar, then, is **shape design and clean colour blocks, multiplied by cheap variants**, and sculpted detail adds nothing to it.

Other shark games point the same way. SharkBite 2's upgrade over the first game was custom models and, above all, **smoother, more realistic animation, with the bite singled out as the biggest change**. Its basic Great White is plain, and the visual interest sits in event and crate skins such as Golden and RGB ([Namu wiki](https://namu.moe/w/%EC%83%BC%EB%B0%942)). The professional mobile franchise does its design work at the form stage too. Hungry Shark's art director gave each shark a **"unique personality, silhouette and style"** in the sculpt, before any paint ([ArtStation](https://ichidan.artstation.com/projects/nY9WmE)). Fan energy follows character, not texture: most of Fisch's fan art came from its event creatures ([DevForum Creator Spotlight](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creator-spotlight-woozynate-makes-a-splash-with-fisch/3269481)).

## Why the smooth Reef Shark keeps reading as "bad"

Reef Shark v2 fixed v1's round-toy blob, but it still has the tells that art-direction sources link to cheap 3D. Its body is lofted with **one constant superellipse exponent**, so every cross-section is a rounded oval. That is the "inflated" look: with no plane changes, every form seems pumped with air. Stylised art works the opposite way. Fortnite's rule is to **"remove all parallel lines"**, and Sea of Thieves' is **"you have to simplify everything"** and build complexity from simple shapes ([Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/08/stylized-graphics-fortnite-sea-of-thieves.html)). Good contours pair a straight against a curve and use only S or C curves ([Brian Lemay](https://brianlemay.com/Drawingcourse/StraightsandCurves.html)). Shape language carries meaning: **triangular shapes signal danger**, and a character that "only reads correctly because of color" has a weak shape system ([RocketBrush](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/shape-language-in-game-character-design-how-to-make-characters-readable-and-consistent)). A faceted model meets all three rules almost automatically, because every face is a plane and every edge loop is a straight segment.

The face is the second failure. v2's mouth is a nearly closed smile with about twenty tiny teeth in a perfect row. At phone size that row blurs into grey noise, and the repetition reads as procedural. Fisch's mouth is open, dark inside and edged with a few big teeth, so it becomes **the highest-contrast spot on the model**. The art-direction research gives the same advice: readability comes from **value contrast first**, and the playable character should be **the brightest, highest-contrast area** in the scene. Too much contrast in too many places confuses the eye ([Icewyrm Games](https://icewyrmgames.github.io/research/reducing-visual-confusion-in-your-game)). Fortnite applies a scale rule along the same lines and keeps gameplay objects no smaller than a mailbox ([Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/08/stylized-graphics-fortnite-sea-of-thieves.html)). v2's black bead eye adds no contrast against a blue sea, where a cyan emissive eye would be the focal point.

Real anatomy is still the skeleton that the stylisation exaggerates. Most sharks have **five gill slits, eight fins, a heterocercal tail whose upper lobe is longer, lateral keels on the tail stock in mackerel sharks, and countershading** ([Wikipedia: Shark anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_anatomy)). Low-poly does not mean anatomy-free. The Megalodon in Fisch keeps its gill slits, fin layout and crescent tail. What changes is that each feature becomes **a few deliberate planes instead of a smooth surface**.

## Choose low-poly faceted, and keep the anatomy rules

The research notes describe two possible routes, and they conflict. The first is the professional "smooth stylised" pipeline: a dense mesh with secondary forms, then a bake onto a low-poly mesh, then a hand-painted texture built from AO, curvature and gradients. It is the route used for stylised characters with Blender and Substance ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/making-stylized-anthropomorphic-shark-adventurer)), and Roblox's flagship creature games use full PBR SurfaceAppearance with emission maps ([Roblox Newsroom, Jul 2026](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/07/roblox-studio-fidelity-creator-interviews-twin-atlas-fluorlite-maximillian-ecos)). The strongest case for it is that it can look richer than Fisch up close. Against it, every hard step in that pipeline (sculpting secondary forms, painting by eye) is exactly where code-built models fall short. It also depends on texture quality reaching the phone, and in March 2026 developers reported **SurfaceAppearance textures loading at very low quality on mobile, even on an iPhone 16 Pro Max**, with the developer linking it to texture streaming and a drop in D1 retention ([DevForum report](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/surface-appearance-loads-inconsistently-on-mobile-and-often-displays-at-very-low-quality-even-if-the-phone-allows-it/4530853)). Texture streaming now picks mip levels from distance, screen size and a memory budget, and 4K is disabled on Android phones under 3.5 GB ([DevForum: Texture Streaming](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-texture-streaming/4144855)).

**The decision is low-poly faceted with flat colour regions, the Fisch route.** It matches the proven top-game reference. It plays to code's strengths, because placing a few hundred vertices exactly and colouring faces by rule is deterministic, whereas painting believable gradients is not. It makes mutations as cheap as Fisch's. It also sidesteps the texture-streaming risk, because the colour lives in the mesh or the part rather than in a map that can arrive blurry. The research from the smooth route still applies wherever it concerns form. Put the mass forward, with the deepest point about one third back from the snout. Make the cross-section a rounded diamond or teardrop, never a circle: a flat wedge at the head, a flatter belly, a hint of a spine ridge, and a tail stock taller than it is wide, with keels. Fins are wedges, thick at the root, sharp at the trailing edge and swept back. Eyes are small, set low on the side and hooded by a brow. Teeth are **few, big, size-varied triangles set into a gum band**, not identical cones in a perfect row. These rules come from combining the cited stylisation principles with anatomy, and they are the core of the shark design rulebook.

Some earlier MEGA BITE documents now need correcting. The style guide's "smooth stylised 3D … smooth shading with soft rim light" and the art note's "never flat plastic" both contradict the decision. They should be rewritten as **"chunky low-poly faceted, flat colour planes, glowing eyes, big toothy grins"**, and the ChatGPT STYLE BLOCK should change too, so that concept turnarounds match the models. The style guide's other rules stand: cute small fish, epic sharks, no blood or scars, a distinct silhouette per evolution.

| Element | Rule for every MEGA BITE shark |
|---|---|
| Shading | Flat (per-face normals); no smooth groups on the body; teeth and fins flat too |
| Body | Forward-weighted loft; diamond/teardrop rings; wedge head with a flat top plane; spine ridge; keel on the tail stock |
| Colour | 2–4 flat regions: dark back, light belly, darker fin tips or one species marking; countershade boundary follows a zig-zag of mesh edges |
| Eye | Small, low, under an angular brow plane; emissive (Neon or emissive mask), species-tinted (cyan default, ember for Megalodon) |
| Mouth | Open at rest by default (about 15–25°); dark plum/maroon interior (not blood red); 6–10 big upper teeth and 5–8 lower, varied size, set in a gum band |
| Gills | 5 dark cut-in slits as geometry or dark faces, in front of the pectorals |
| Fins | Angular plates with real thickness at the root; dorsal tall triangle; pectorals long and swept; crescent tail, upper lobe larger |

## A code-only Blender generator in nine steps

The generator should replace `reef_shark_v2.py` with one parametric "genome" per species, an idea borrowed from Infinigen, which builds creatures (fish among them) from a parameter tree of parts lofted along a centre curve and is **BSD-3 licensed, so its code can be read and adapted with attribution** ([Infinigen paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310); [GitHub](https://github.com/princeton-vl/infinigen)). Steps 1 and 2 lock proportions and build the cage. Load a side and a top reference silhouette, drawn by us or generated as a ChatGPT concept turnaround in the new faceted style, never a copy of Fisch art. Threshold each image to a mask and read the top, bottom and half-width per column. Build the cage directly from about **14–18 rings of 8–10 vertices**, not by subdividing a smooth loft. Each ring's vertex layout is designed: one vertex on the spine ridge, two shoulders, two flanks, one belly-edge pair and a flat belly. That gives **real planes and a ridge instead of an oval**. Step 3 adds the head and jaw as hand-placed vertex groups: the flat top plane, the brow edge over the eye, a straight jaw line, and a split loop at the hinge so the lower jaw opens. Step 4 builds the fins as angular plates. Define each fin by three outline curves (leading edge, trailing edge, root chord), give it real thickness tapering to a single trailing edge, and sink the root 10–20% into the body. Roblox requires watertight meshes with real volume, so fins cannot be zero-thickness cards ([Roblox: Mesh specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/specifications)).

Step 5 builds the teeth and mouth. Place teeth along the jaw curve with Geometry Nodes or plain bmesh (curve-to-points, instance, align to tangent, random scale 0.8–1.1, slight tilt), using flat triangular blades of 4–6 triangles each, then realise and join. The mouth interior is a closed dark cavity. Step 6 assigns colour per face. The rule is computed per face: back or belly from the face centre's height relative to a per-ring threshold, with alternating triangles along the boundary ring to make the zig-zag line. Fin tips switch by distance along the fin span, and the eye, teeth, gums and mouth interior get their own region IDs. Step 7 flattens the shading: call `shade_flat()` and export per-face normals, so Roblox sees hard facets. Step 8 builds the rig: **8–10 spine and tail bones** (shorter toward the tail), plus jaw, pectoral and caudal-lobe bones. Weights are set by distance along the spine, limited to four influences and normalised, with the root bone at the origin and no influence on it. These are Roblox's hard rules: **at most 4 influences per vertex, frozen bones, 20,000 triangles per mesh, one animation per FBX export** ([Roblox: Mesh specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/specifications)). v2's 5-bone spine is too coarse for a smooth swim curve. Step 9 exports with Apply Scalings set to FBX Unit Scale, Add Leaf Bones off, and Bake Animation only for the clip being exported ([Roblox: Export requirements](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/export-requirements)). Swim, Bite and the ability clip are exported separately.

The export format follows from how colour is stored. For the **player sharks, export one mesh per colour region** (body-back, belly, fins, mouth and gums, teeth, eyes), all skinned to the same armature, and colour them in Studio with plain MeshPart.Color and SmoothPlastic, with Neon on the eyes. This copies Fisch's per-part method exactly. Mutations become a Luau colour and material preset (Golden, Albino, Frozen glass, Phantom translucent-neon, Ember) plus a ParticleEmitter aura, recoloured at runtime with no new assets. For **NPC fish**, use a single mesh with vertex colours, which the FBX importer carries unless "Ignore Vertex Colors" is set ([Roblox: 3D Importer](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/3d-importer)). If a shark ever needs a texture, use a small palette map with an **EmissiveMask on the eyes**. SurfaceAppearance supports emissive mask, strength and tint, and its Color multiplier tints at no extra cost ([Roblox Docs: PBR textures](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/surface-appearance)). The catch is that SurfaceAppearance cannot be edited by scripts at runtime, so variants must be prepared in advance ([same](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/surface-appearance)). That limit is why the per-part route wins for player sharks. One caveat: the multi-mesh skinned import has not yet been tested in our place, so the Reef Shark rebuild must confirm it before the other sharks follow.

## Roblox settings and budgets for phones

The budget drops sharply from v2's 8,920 triangles, because facets only work when each face is large enough to read as a plane. These are our design targets, not measured Fisch numbers (no public polycounts exist).

| Asset | Triangles | Colour | Bones | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Player shark (Reef–Great White) | 1,500–3,000 | 4–6 region meshes, flat Color + Neon eye | 8–10 spine + jaw + pectorals + tail | Teeth ~300–500 of that |
| Megalodon / boss | 3,000–6,000 | as above + emissive accent strips | as above | Seen from 20–60 studs; teeth and eye must read far |
| NPC predator | 800–1,500 | vertex colours | 4–5 or script-driven | |
| Prey fish | 150–600 | vertex colours, shared presets | 0–3 | Wiggle by script or a shared clip |

Several Roblox settings follow from the research. **Skinned meshes get no automatic LOD**, a limitation staff confirmed ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/skinned-mesh-rig-cannot-generate-lod-on-any-fidelity/1731085)), so distant fish need a client-side swap to a static or hidden mesh. Mobile NPC slowdowns usually come from network ownership and default Animate scripts, not triangles. One test ran about 30 skinned NPCs that lagged until those were removed, while hundreds of script-less NPCs ran fine ([DevForum case study](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/game-with-skinned-meshes-has-poor-performance-on-mobile-renderfidelity-doing-nothing/2649641)). Lighting must be designed for the worst case. Future lighting reached Android in 2024, but it is "costly in performance", and low and mid-range devices get reduced lighting quality automatically ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/future-is-bright-on-android-is-fully-rolled-out-client-beta/3235808)). The current controls are `LightingStyle`, `PrioritizeLightingQuality` and the environment diffuse and specular scales ([Roblox Docs: Lighting](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/lighting)). Flat-coloured countershading plus an emissive eye reads well even with no shadows at all, which is a further argument for this style. Use Bloom with a high threshold, so that only eyes, vents and abilities glow, in line with the style guide's lighting rule. Use **Highlight outlines only on the player's own shark or a boss**: each highlighted object adds a draw call, and any visible Highlight triggers a full-screen post-process ([DevForum: More Highlights](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/lights-camera-more-highlights/4061534)). Set collision to Box or Hull and give bites a simple invisible hitbox. Avoid DoubleSided except on true single-sided cards ([DevForum: DoubleSided](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/meshpart-doublesided/864664)).

## An automatic gate that rejects "bad" before the owner sees it

Research on AI-driven Blender modelling found that a critic reviewing screenshots improved proportions and materials. It also found that **quality plateaus or degrades after about three critique iterations**, and that small idempotent scripts with fixed object names work best ([arXiv 2601.05016](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05016v1)). The gate is therefore a fixed render set plus hard numbers plus at most three fix rounds, keeping the best version. Every build renders the same views under the same lights: side, top, front, three-quarter front-high (the chase camera), head close-up with the mouth open, swim-bend extremes, and **a 128 px thumbnail on the game's sea colour**. It also renders a flat-grey version, to test form without colour.

The numeric checks carry the weight. A **side and top silhouette IoU against the reference above 0.9** catches proportion drift. A **facet check** catches any slide back to "smooth": every face flat-shaded, a median angle between neighbouring face normals on the body of roughly 10–25°, and no body face smaller than about 0.3% of body length. A **palette check** counts at most four body colour regions plus eye, teeth and mouth, each with a distinct value. It also requires the back-to-belly value difference and the teeth-against-mouth contrast to stay large when the 128 px render is converted to greyscale, which is the desaturation test used in game readability work ([Icewyrm Games](https://icewyrmgames.github.io/research/reducing-visual-confusion-in-your-game)). A **face check** confirms that the eye material is emissive and is the brightest pixel cluster in the thumbnail, that the mouth is open at least about 15° at rest with 6–10 upper teeth visible from the side, and that no two neighbouring teeth have identical size and rotation. **Mesh health** requires zero non-manifold edges, triangles within budget, at most 4 influences per vertex with weights summing to 1, and no self-intersection at the swim and bite extremes (a BVH overlap test). Only after every number passes does a vision-model critique score silhouette, face appeal and "does it look Roblox-native or AI-made" from 1 to 5. The final judge is a side-by-side render next to a Fisch Megalodon frame, used as a quality reference only, plus the quality-nerd kid simulation. The thresholds are our engineering choices and should be tuned after the first Reef Shark passes or fails visually.

## Conclusion

The deeper lesson is that "quality" in Roblox depends on what looks native to the platform. Fisch looks premium because its sharks are **honest low-poly objects with confident planes, two clean colours, one glowing eye and a mouth full of teeth**, and that is a target code can hit precisely, which smooth stylised sculpts are not. The recommended plan has four steps. First, rewrite the style guide and STYLE BLOCK to the faceted look. Second, rebuild the Reef Shark as the template generator (designed ring layout, angular head and brow, open mouth with 8 big upper teeth, cyan emissive eye, grey-blue over white with a zig-zag line and black fin tips, a 10-bone rig with Swim and Bite clips), pass the gate, import it into Studio as region meshes, confirm per-part recolour and Neon eyes on a real low-RAM Android phone, and show the owner the v1 render before any polish. Third, derive the family from the same genome in risk order. The **Great White** comes first: crescent tail with keel, abrupt grey-white line, black "armpit" spot, big serrated teeth ([Florida Museum](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/white-shark/)). The **Megalodon** is the same family but longer and slimmer ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/megalodon-largest-shark-fossil-long-body)), navy with ember-glow eyes, armour-like ridge planes and giant teeth, and gets the most effort because boss creatures drive fan attention. Next comes the **Hammerhead**, with a flat cephalofoil about 23–27% of body length, eyes on the ends and a very tall sickle dorsal ([Wikipedia: Great hammerhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_hammerhead)). Last come the three fish on a simpler, rounder variant of the generator: **Barracuda** (projecting lower jaw, fangs, two separated dorsals, dark bars) ([New World Encyclopedia](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Barracuda)), **Piranha** (deep body, interlocking underbite), and **Minnow** (cute, big eye, silver with a side stripe). Fourth, build each evolution's mutations as Luau colour and material presets plus particle auras, never new meshes, and put the saved effort into the bite animation, which made the biggest visible difference in SharkBite 2.

The main uncertainty is whether a few screenshots captured everything about Fisch's look. Material settings, polycounts and any subtle vertex-colour gradients are still unknown. The gate's numbers are a starting calibration, not a proven standard. Neither matters much for the decision: the style choice is clear, and the first rebuilt Reef Shark, judged next to a Fisch frame on a real phone, will show within one iteration whether the generator's facet size and palette need adjusting.
