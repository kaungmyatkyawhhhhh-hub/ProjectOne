## What creatures appear in Hungry Shark Evolution/World and similar games, and what role does each play?

### Takeaway
Hungry Shark games build a "size-gated hazard" ecosystem: most creatures are food, but a set of medium hazards (puffer fish, lionfish, jellyfish, stingrays, anglerfish, seal pups, divers) punish small sharks and become food once you reach a higher tier; mines, submarines and special sharks act as the real threats/bosses. Maneater adds a clearer "prey / threat / apex boss" ladder, where each area's apex boss is a timed dodge-then-strike fight that unlocks an evolution.

### Cited Findings
**Hungry Shark Evolution / World (Ubisoft/Future Games of London)**
- Puffer Fish: a "medium-level danger" fish, one of the most common fish in the game. It stays deflated until a shark comes close; in Evolution it inflates the second time a shark passes without eating it, in World it inflates as soon as a shark is in range, so the player must boost and eat it first. A cloaking device stops it inflating — [Hungry Shark Wiki: Puffer Fish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Puffer_Fish)
- Puffer Fish eating rules: deflated = any shark can eat it; inflated = only Tiger Shark and above (Evolution), L/XL sharks and above in World (wiki is inconsistent). Biting it inflated with Reef/Mako/Hammerhead costs 10 HP. Eating it gives 90 points and restores 15 HP; World plays a loud "deflating" sound. Pop-up accolades: "Puff Pastry!", "Blow Out!", and on failure "Aquapuncture!", "Prickly!" — [Hungry Shark Wiki: Puffer Fish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Puffer_Fish)
- In World the puffer looks like a plain yellow fish and was once confused with the green Gem Fish until the Gem Fish design was changed to tell them apart (a readability lesson) — [Hungry Shark Wiki: Puffer Fish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Puffer_Fish)
- Lionfish: passive until disturbed, then visibly grows larger and shows its poisonous spines; returns to calm once the shark leaves range. Each sting = 15 HP in one burst (contrast: jellyfish drain health gradually). Harmless to and edible by Hammerhead+ (Evolution) / L sharks+ (World); smaller sharks can eat them only during Gold Rush. Found near land, rock gaps, currents and caves, in groups of 4–6+; in World, Pacific Islands — [Hungry Shark Wiki: Lionfish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Lionfish)
- Player-view hazard list (TV Tropes, HSE): mines (knockback can chain into other mines; Ultra Mines instantly kill anything smaller than a Great White); other sharks (Reef/Hammerhead stop mattering as you grow, Great Whites kill in a few bites); Pyro Shark (flamethrower, you cannot eat while burning; called the most dangerous shark, appears early); submarines (three torpedoes with knockback; disable by boosting into the cockpit); green and pink jellyfish (drain even large sharks fast); blue jellyfish (weaker); lionfish (not edible until Hammerhead, bumping into them breaks momentum); anglerfish (weak bites but hard to counter at depth); seal pups (rare, in packs, latch on and block eating, restore lots of health when eaten); scuba divers (red = speargun, green = knife combos); stingrays (pause, then tail-sting when approached) — [TV Tropes: Hungry Shark Evolution YMMV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/YMMV/HungrySharkEvolution)
- Tier gating of hazards: Megalodon can eat regular and Mega Mines; sharks above that tier can eat Ultra Mines and jellyfish; Pyro Shark only edible by Big Daddy or larger. Players call mines/subs "Demonic Spiders" and the small hazards "Goddamned Bats" (annoying, not deadly) — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/YMMV/HungrySharkEvolution)
- Other HSE wiki entries confirmed to exist (search results): Anglerfish, Sailfish, Human, Giant Crab (boss-like fight seen in a Megalodon video), Evil Whale Shark, stingrays (a video titled "Reef Shark Getting Killed by Stingrays"), Gulp Fish — [Anglerfish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Anglerfish), [Sailfish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Sailfish), [Giant Crab video](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/File:Hungry_Shark_Evolution_Defeating_Giant_Crab_With_Megalodon), [Evil Whale Shark](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Evil_Whale_Shark), [Stingrays video](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/File:Reef_Shark_Getting_Killed_by_Stingrays)

### Inferences
- Core pattern to copy: every hazard has (1) a visible "tell" (puffer inflates, lionfish spreads spines, stingray pauses, musky hisses), (2) a size tier at which it flips from hazard to food, and (3) a reward for eating it once you can (puffer heals 15 HP + 90 pts). This gives small players a reason to want the next shark ("then I can eat puffers").
- Distinct damage styles make hazards readable: burst (lionfish), damage-over-time (jellyfish), lock/latch that blocks eating (seal pups), knockback (mines/torpedoes), "can't eat while affected" status (Pyro burn). For a kids' game, swap burn/torpedo/speargun for non-violent equivalents: electric zap (eel), ink cloud (squid/octopus blinds screen), sting tingle (jellyfish), spiky puff (puffer bounce-back).
- Feed and Grow: Fish (general knowledge, not verified): grow-by-eating sandbox where every fish can eat anything smaller and you can see other fish's size; the key lesson is a visible size comparison.
- Depth (Digital Confectioners, general knowledge): sharks vs divers PvP; Abzu (Giant Squid, general knowledge): non-combat ocean with large boids schools and huge ambient creatures, the strongest reference for "alive" ambient life; Subnautica (general knowledge): Reaper Leviathan = apex fear made by sound and silhouette long before the creature is seen; peaceful giants (Reefback, Sea Emperor) for awe.
- Roblox: SharkBite is the best-known Roblox shark game (sharks vs boats) — specific creature roster not researched.

## Which real creatures make the best food tiers, mid threats and apex/boss threats, and why?

### Takeaway
Use a size ladder where each tier's typical prey is about a quarter to half the size of the player shark and each tier unlocks one former hazard as food; pick creatures whose silhouettes are instantly different (round puffer, flat ray, long eel, many-armed octopus, tall-finned marlin, hammer head) so kids can read size and danger at a glance.

### Cited Findings
- Bigger prey = more health/nutrients but harder to eat; once grown it is "eaten whole in one bite" (scaling food value with prey size) — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2020/05/22/6-creatures-meet-then-eat-maneater-xbox/)
- Abundant small prey acts as the "emergency heal" — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2020/05/22/6-creatures-meet-then-eat-maneater-xbox/)
- Two creatures that look alike (yellow puffer vs green Gem Fish) confused players until one was redesigned: silhouettes/colours must differ per role — [Hungry Shark Wiki](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Puffer_Fish)
- Great white shark: largest predatory fish, average 4.5 m, females over 6 m and up to 2.5 tonnes; prey includes seals, sea lions, small whales and sea turtles — [Penguin Kids: Sea creature facts](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- Striped marlin: over 3 m, bursts up to 80 km/h, flashes iridescent blue stripes when excited to confuse prey (a great "speed dash" mid-predator with a visual tell) — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- Bigfin reef squid: 4–33 cm, fastest-growing large marine invertebrate (full size in about 4 months), changes colour and pattern for camouflage — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- Pilot fish: swims with sharks and rays, gets protection and keeps the predator clean of parasites (mutualism) — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)

## What behaviours make sea life feel ALIVE?

### Takeaway
Life comes from schools that move as one (boids: separation, alignment, cohesion plus a "flee predator" force), from creatures reacting to the player with visible tells (puff, flash, hide, ink), and from non-edible ambient life (coral, anemones, krill clouds, distant whales, light shafts) that fills the world.

### Cited Findings
- Boids (Craig Reynolds, 1986/1987 SIGGRAPH "Flocks, Herds and Schools") is the standard flocking model; used for ambient wildlife including "sea creatures" in games — [O'Reilly: AI for Game Developers ch.4 Flocking](https://oreilly.com/library/view/ai-for-game/0596005555/ch04.html) (chapter intro/ToC only; section headings: Steering Model, Neighbors, Cohesion, Alignment, Separation, Obstacle Avoidance, Follow the Leader); canonical page [Reynolds: Boids](http://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/) (redirect prevented fetch); overview [ALife Encyclopedia: Boids](https://alife.org/encyclopedia/software-platforms/boids/) (search result only).
- Hazards in HSE have reactive states (puffer inflates on approach; lionfish grows and shows spines when disturbed, then calms when you leave) — [Puffer Fish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Puffer_Fish), [Lionfish](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Lionfish)
- Audio tells and flash windows (musky hisses; creatures flash when vulnerable) — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2020/05/22/6-creatures-meet-then-eat-maneater-xbox/)
- Interactive prey as toys (Maneater turtle whipshot) — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2020/05/22/6-creatures-meet-then-eat-maneater-xbox/)
- Pilot fish follow sharks (real behaviour; perfect cosmetic "followers" around big players) — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)

### Inferences
- Boids recipe for Roblox (general knowledge): each fish steers by (1) separation — avoid crowding within ~1 body length; (2) alignment — match average heading of neighbours within a radius, often limited to a forward field of view (~270°); (3) cohesion — steer toward neighbours' centre; add (4) flee — strong vector away from any player shark bigger than the fish within a fear radius; (5) bounds/obstacle avoidance; (6) a weak wander/goal target. Performance: update schools on the client, use a spatial grid for neighbour lookups, update far schools at lower rates, and replicate only a seed + school centre from the server (server owns "which fish was eaten").
- Bait-ball behaviour: when a predator approaches, a school compresses into a ball and opens a hole around the predator ("fountain effect"); this looks spectacular and is easy with boids (raise cohesion, add flee).
- Idle animations that sell life: fin flutter, gill pulses, occasional darting, eels breathing in holes, crabs waving claws, rays flapping wings, octopus colour shimmer, jellyfish bell pulse, seahorses bobbing.
- Reactions to the player: small fish scatter; mid fish flee only from bigger sharks and chase smaller ones (size-relative AI); cleaner fish/pilot fish follow large players; clownfish hide in anemones; octopus ink-and-dash; puffer puffs; squid flashes colours.
- Non-edible ambient: coral polyps swaying, kelp, anemones, sea fans, bubbles, god rays, plankton motes, distant whale song, dolphins leaping at the surface, seabirds diving into bait balls, bioluminescent plankton trails at night.

## Which creatures are best for "gentle giant" ambience?

### Takeaway
Whale shark is the strongest pick: world's largest fish, harmless, unique spot patterns, a kid-famous fact set; pair it with manta rays, humpback whales, sea turtles and blue whale silhouettes as awe-moments, not food.

### Cited Findings
- Whale shark: longest widely accepted length ~18.8 m (62 ft); one reported at 34 tonnes; eats shrimp, fish eggs, krill, crab larvae, small fish like anchovies; throat only about the size of a baseball; each has a unique spot pattern like a fingerprint (17,000+ identified); tagged sharks travelled 3,414 and 4,275 km; deepest recorded dive ~1,978 m; skin up to 15 cm thick (thickest of any animal); harmless to people; may live 80–130 years; warm seas ~30°N–30°S; Endangered — [Marine Megafauna Foundation: Whale shark facts](https://marinemegafauna.org/whale-sharks/facts)
- Whale shark often over 18 m, feeds almost exclusively on plankton and tiny fish, "harmless to most creatures" — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- HSE turned the whale shark into a villain ("Evil Whale Shark") — [Hungry Shark Wiki](https://hungry-shark.fandom.com/wiki/Evil_Whale_Shark)

### Inferences
- Manta ray (wingspan up to ~7 m, general knowledge): graceful wing flaps, barrel rolls while feeding, visit "cleaning stations", leap out of the water; the largest brains of any fish. Great as rideable/escort ambience.
- Humpback whale (~15 m): songs, breaching, bubble-net feeding (a spiral of bubbles to trap fish) — a spectacular scripted event.
- Blue whale (~25–30 m, largest animal ever): use as a rare passing silhouette with deep calls.
- Sea turtles (green ~1–1.2 m; leatherback up to ~2 m, dives over 1 km, eats jellyfish): slow, calm, flee gently; a jellyfish-clearing helper. Kid-friendly rule: make turtles, dolphins, manatees, whale sharks non-edible "friends" (avoid upset parents; also gives a "protect the gentle giants" vibe).
- Basking shark (~8–10 m, mouth open filter feeder), dolphins (play in bow waves, echolocation), manatee/dugong (seagrass grazers) also fit.
- A gentle giant could be a mobile "safe zone" or buff aura (pilot-fish idea reversed: small players swimming beside the whale shark are protected) — ties to Zev's safe zone/PvP zone design in memory.

## Real facts kids love (Fish Index material)

### Takeaway
Kids respond to "biggest / fastest / weirdest / glows / changes colour / dad has the babies" facts; ocean animals are perennial favourites, with sharks, dolphins, octopus, orcas, whale sharks and sea turtles topping fan lists (non-scientific polls).

### Cited Findings
- The ocean holds between half and three-quarters of all life on Earth — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- Male seahorses carry the young in a belly pouch; seahorses mate for life — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- Walrus tusks nearly 1 m long, used to haul out and break ice holes — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- Whale shark spots are unique like fingerprints; throat the size of a baseball; skin 15 cm thick — [Marine Megafauna Foundation](https://marinemegafauna.org/whale-sharks/facts)
- Great white: biggest predatory fish, eats floating dead whales — [Penguin Kids](https://penguin.co.uk/articles/childrens-article/facts-about-sea-creatures-for-kids)
- Popularity lists exist (TheTopTens "Top 10 Favorite Sea Creatures", "Best Ocean Animals", "Cutest Sea-Dwelling Animals") — fan-voted, not scientific; pages not fetched — [TheTopTens: Favorite Sea Creatures](https://www.thetoptens.com/most-favorite-sea-creatures/), [Cutest sea animals](https://www.thetoptens.com/cutest-adorable-sea-dwelling-animals/)

### Inferences (fact ideas, general knowledge — verify before shipping)
- Octopus: three hearts, blue blood, can change colour and skin texture in under a second, can squeeze through any hole bigger than its beak.
- Sharks: have been around ~450 million years (older than trees); lose and regrow thousands of teeth; can sense electricity (ampullae of Lorenzini); a hammerhead's wide head helps find stingrays buried in sand.
- Mako: fastest shark; sailfish: often called the fastest fish.
- Greenland shark: may live ~400 years.
- Pufferfish: puffs up by gulping water; some male pufferfish draw sand circle "art" to attract mates.
- Parrotfish: sleeps in a mucus bubble; its poop makes white sand beaches.
- Mantis shrimp: punches as fast as a bullet, sees more colours than humans.
- Electric eel: not really an eel; zaps up to ~860 V.
- Anglerfish: the lure glows thanks to bacteria.
- Blue whale: heart as big as a small car, the largest animal ever.
- Clownfish: all are born male; protected by anemone mucus.
- Sea turtles: return to the beach where they hatched to lay eggs.
- Dolphins: sleep with half the brain at a time; have names (signature whistles).
- Starfish: no brain, no blood; can regrow arms.
- Most deep-sea animals make their own light (bioluminescence); the commonest glow colour is blue-green because blue light travels farthest in water.

- Food chain tooltip in Fish Index: plankton → krill/small fish → mid fish (tuna, barracuda) → sharks → orca; whale sharks/blue whales skip the middle and eat plankton directly.
