## What Titan Simulator's evolutions were
**Not creatures. Each evolution is an armour BODY SET on the normal Roblox avatar.** The body (torso, arms, legs) is swapped; the set has no head, so your own head, face and hat stay on top. The game called them "Armors".

## Why it works (rules read from the 30)
1. **It is always YOU.** Your head, face and hat never change. Kids recognise themselves and their friends at any size.
2. **One skeleton.** Every set fits the same avatar rig, so every Roblox animation and emote works. No custom rig, no custom clips.
3. **Variety of THEME, not one species:** knights, dark lords, flame, skeleton, robot, golem, angel wings, pumpkin, dragon, werewolf, robed lich, stone warrior, reaper. The next one is a surprise.
4. **The outline changes through add-ons:** shoulder pads, spikes, wings, robes, tails, claws.
5. **Names are character titles:** "The Doombringer", "Lord Umberhallow", "The Reaper".
6. **Dense early, wide late:** 0, 30, 70, 140, 240, 350, 500, 750, 1k ... then 100k, 150k, 200k, 300k, 500k, 1M. Nine evolutions inside the first 1,000 power.
7. **30 of them.** We planned 8 before the first boss; armour sets are cheap enough to plan many more.

## What I got wrong
- I designed a new creature species. The reference the owner named has no creatures at all. The Venom study said the same thing (the monster grows ON your avatar) and I still jumped to full monsters at form 3.
- Faceted low-poly creature renders are a different art world from Roblox avatars. "Looks Roblox" = the avatar.

## Stomp Evolution, corrected: every form is an armour body set on YOUR avatar
- All forms are worn on the standard Roblox avatar (R15). Head, face, hair and hats stay the player's own.
- Our signature stays: every set has OVERSIZED STOMPING BOOTS, and the boots leave that form's footprint.
- The element ladder stays (stone, iron, magma, storm, crystal, star) and themes can now go anywhere each week (robot, dragon, pumpkin for Halloween...).
- Growth: the whole avatar scales up; each set is also bulkier than the last.
- **Our own designs, not Roblox's bundles.** Titan Simulator used Roblox's own catalog bundles. That is the fastest route, but every copycat has the same ones, they cannot be "ours" on the icon, and the owner's rule is own models. Default: we make our own sets. Owner can overrule.
- Build cost drops a lot: about 15 rigid body parts per set fitted to one rig, standard animations, no custom skeleton. The monster rig, the clip set and the head sheets are no longer needed.
- The bosses (JUNK TITAN, the ROGUE) can stay non-avatar; the ROGUE should become a giant avatar in a corrupted set.

## Proportion fix (owner 11 Nov 09:56: "why the legs look big compared to the body? ingame it wont look like that right")
- Cause: my "oversized stomping boots, 1.5 to 2 times the leg width" instruction. ChatGPT thickened the whole lower leg, so the three sheets above are bottom-heavy. A model traced from them WOULD look like that in game, and wide legs clip into each other in the walk animation.
- **Rule from now on: real avatar proportions.** Each leg is half the torso width (two legs = the torso), legs as tall as the torso, arms as wide as a leg. Armour adds only a thin layer on the legs. Boots at most a quarter wider than the leg and no higher than the ankle. The bulk goes on the shoulders, chest and forearms, like Titan Simulator's armours (top-heavy, heroic).
- **"Oversized boots as the signature" is dropped.** The stomp signature moves to the footprint and the stomp effect, not the model's shape.
- Corrected sheet: `stomp_sheet_stage3_stone_v2_normal_legs_preview.png` (full size in Downloads as `stomp_sheet_stage3_stone_v2_normal_legs.png`). The first three sheets and line-up v4 are superseded on proportions; redo stages 1-2 and 8 with the same proportion paragraph.
- Prompt paragraph that fixed it: "Proportion rules, follow them exactly: the torso is a box; each leg is a straight box exactly HALF as wide as the torso, so the two legs side by side are exactly as wide as the torso, with only a thin gap between them; the legs are the same height as the torso; each arm is a straight box the same width as one leg ... The stone armour only adds a thin layer on top of these boxes ... The BOOTS are only slightly bigger than the leg: about one quarter wider and no higher than the ankle ... Put the bulk on the UPPER body instead ... top-heavy and heroic, not bottom-heavy."

**Texture rules that worked (reuse per material):** fine grain, chips and hairline cracks; a lighter worn highlight painted along every edge; darker dirt in every crevice and under every overlap; colour variation from plate to plate; stitching on leather; brushed scratches and a bevel highlight on metal; inner facets in crystals; glow = a white-hot centre line with a soft bloom.
**What this changes in the build (different from the MEGA BITE sharks, which were flat colours in one 512 texture):**
- Each set needs painted texture maps: colour, normal, roughness, metalness (Roblox SurfaceAppearance on MeshParts).
- Vault rule so far: Roblox stores images at most 1024 px wide. So split a set into about 5 mesh parts (torso, two arms, two legs, plus shoulder/back add-ons), each with its own 1024 texture, to keep the detail sharp. Check the current limit before the first export.
- Source: tileable material textures from ChatGPT swatches, made seamless and turned into normal/roughness maps (the art pipeline skill already covers this), then baked onto the model in Blender with edge wear and crevice dirt from the model's own shape.
- Glow parts stay separate Neon/emissive parts so they bloom in game.

## Human proportions (owner 11 Nov 10:21: "now theres no feet. learn the proportions of a human body")
I swung from feet too big (v1, v4) to no feet (v5). Looked the numbers up instead of guessing.
**Human proportions (figure-drawing canon, sources below):**
| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Height | 7.5 to 8 heads for an adult |
| Middle of the body | at the pelvis: the legs are half the height |
| Knee | halfway between the hip and the sole |
| Foot length | about one head; the same as the forearm (elbow to wrist) |
| Foot width | about one third of its length |
| Foot height | about one third of a head |
| Shoulders | 2 to 3 head widths |
| Hand | as long as the face |
**Applied to our avatar (the Roblox head is oversized, so use body parts as the ruler, not the head):**
- Legs about 1.5 times the torso (shoulder to hip); knee at the middle; leg half the torso width, a little narrower at the ankle.
- **Foot = forearm length.** It sticks out in front of the shin by about one shin-width, with a small heel. Height about a third of its length, width about a third of its length (only a little wider than the ankle). Never wider than the knee, never missing.
- Elbow at the waist, fists at mid-thigh. Shoulders about 1.5 times the hips.
Sheet v6: `stomp_sheet_stage3_stone_v6_human_proportions_preview.png` (full size in Downloads as `stomp_sheet_stage3_stone_v6_human_proportions.png`). Front, side and back at one size + the boot from the side, front and above. **v6 replaces v5 as the reference for every set.**
