# Research - Myanmar licence plates (for the Yangon driving game)

Short version: a Yangon private car plate is **black with white letters**. It has three lines: `YGN-38` (region + township number) on top, `5T-1648` big in the middle, and the car make/model at the bottom. Taxis are **red** with doubled letters (`HH-7891`). Plates have been Latin since Sept 2013. Myanmar people value the digit **9**, repeated digits, and digit sums of 9. I found **no reported RTAD plate auction prices**. The only real plate price I found is 500,000 kyat for "7704" (2013). Phone-number auctions give the best price evidence for patterns.

## 1. Current format

- Run by the Road Transport Administration Department (RTAD). [W]
- Current format since **1 July 2021**. [W] [AD]
- **Top line:** 3-letter region code + dash + township number, e.g. `YGN-38` (Yangon, Dagon township). [W] [AC]
- **Middle line (big):** serial = digit + letter, dash, 4 digits, e.g. `1P-3574`, `3G-7224`. [W]
- **Bottom line:** car make and model, e.g. `HONDA FIT`, `SUZUKI ERTIGA`. [W] Added in 2021 so a plate can't be moved to another car. [AD]
- Small extras: tiny "RTA" under the dash; QR code + small serial number top left (seen on the drawn 2021 sample; QR **unconfirmed** on real plates). [C1]
- **2013-2021 format:** top line is the region code only (`MDY`, `YGN`), no township number. [EP] [C2]
- Region codes: YGN Yangon, MDY Mandalay, NPW Naypyitaw (not "NPT"), AYY, BGO, CHN, KYH, KCN, KYN, MGY, MON, RKE, SGG, SHN, TNI. [W] [AC]
- Yangon township numbers run 1-45. Useful downtown ones: 9 Tamwe, 16 Pazundaung, 17 Botahtaung, 18 Mingala Taungnyunt, 20 Yankin, 33 Kamayut, 34 Kyauktada, 36 Sanchaung, 38 Dagon, 39 Pabedan, 40 Bahan, 41 Mayangone, 42 Latha, 43 Lanmadaw, 44 Hlaing, 45 Ahlone. Also 3 Mingaladon, 8 Insein, 31 Thanlyin. (My transliterations of the Burmese list.) [AC]

### How the series runs
- One shared pool for private, government and police cars. The next number can go to any of them. [EP]
- Pattern: `1A-0001 ... 1A-9999`, then the next letter. Prefix digit 1-9, letter A-Z.
- RTAD's official "latest numbers reached" list, **17 Nov 2026**: [RT]
  - `5T` 1648 (main car series)
  - `HH` 7891 (doubled letters = taxi / bus series)
  - `8M` 8859, `9V` 90860 (5 digits), `89W` 99998, `32U` 8521 (other categories)
  - `3TLR` 4160 (trailers), `HSE` 6065 (hearses)
  - What `8M`, `9V`, `89W`, `32U` stand for is **unconfirmed**. Wikipedia shows Mandalay motorcycles as "W" and three-wheelers as "U", and tractors with 5 digits, so likely: W = motorcycle, U = three-wheeler, 5-digit = tractor/machinery. **Unconfirmed.**
- Real examples seen: 1P, 3G, 3M, 6H, 8L, 9F. Since 9F (2019) is older than 1P (2021) and 5T (2026), the series likely **looped back** after 9Z. **Unconfirmed.**
- **Skipped letters:** no reliable source. Europlate saw I and Q in use. RTAD's own list shows `9V`, so V is used. One wiki says I, O, V are skipped, but it has no sources and RTAD contradicts it. Treat as **unconfirmed**; for the game, skipping I and O is safe and readable.
- **Taxi / bus series:** doubled letters `AA ... HH` with 1-9999. [EP] [W] Currently at `HH`. [RT] Before 2013 the same idea in Burmese letters: ကက, ခခ. [IR]

### Older formats
- Before 1948: Latin plates from Rangoon, prefixes RA-RD. [EP]
- ~1955 to 2013: Burmese script plates, about 60 years. [EP]
- Before 1999: hand-painted. 1999: embossed Burmese plates. [AD]
- **Sept 2013:** switched to Latin letters and Arabic digits (for the SEA Games). [AD] [W]
- 2019: Latin embossed plates with security features for all types except trailers/machinery; trailers in July 2020. [AD]
- Wa State uses its own W-series plates. [W]

## 2. Burmese vs Latin numerals
- Current cars: **Latin only** (letters + 0-9). [AD] [W]
- Old (pre-2013) plates: Burmese numerals and letters, e.g. ၁၂၃၄. [W]
- In 2017, **motorcycles and military** still used Burmese script, e.g. motorcycle "34 Ya 44226". [EP] The army photo above shows Burmese numerals ၉၉၀၀(၀) and a star. [C5]
- Some old commercial vehicles carry "translation" plates (old Burmese number rewritten in Latin). [EP]

## 4. Size, font and style
- Size: **358 x 162 mm** (ratio about 2.2:1). Not the EU 520x110. [W]
- Pressed (embossed) aluminium, 1 mm thick. [IR]
- Raised rim border, raised characters (white painted on the raised part). [C2] [C3]
- Three lines in the 2021 format: region-township small on top, serial large, make/model small at bottom. [W] [C1]
- Tiny "RTAD" security text repeated across the plate; round seal stamps in the top corners; two bolts at the top. [C2] [C3] [EP]
- Font: plain condensed sans-serif, all caps. Exact font name **unconfirmed**. DIN-like condensed works.
- No national emblem or flag on the plate. MYA oval is a separate sticker for cross-border trips. [RA]
- Plate fee: K40,000 per embossed car plate from 1 Jan 2026 (was K9,000). Motorcycles K7,000. [GN]

## 5. Lucky numbers and prices

### What Myanmar people value
- **9 is the luckiest digit.** Plates and phone numbers with 9 are "in high demand". Linked to the nine attributes of the Buddha. [GT]
- **Digit sums of 9** are prized. Example: "43 98 76 53" is popular because the digits sum to 9. [GT]
- General Ne Win's 45 and 90 kyat notes (1987) were made for his lucky 9. [GT] [NW]
- **37** is also coveted (the 37 nats). [GT]
- **Repeated digits** win every Myanmar number auction (see phone prices below). [NT] [IW]
- **8:** said to be lucky (Eight-fold Noble Path). Weak source. [LG] Chinese-style "8 = wealth" influence: **unconfirmed** for Myanmar.
- "8888" is also the date of the 8 Aug 1988 uprising. Politically sensitive; avoid making it a joke item.
- **Unlucky digits:** no source found. **Unconfirmed.**
- **Low numbers (0001) and sequences (1234):** only an unsourced wiki says these sell high. **Unconfirmed.**
- **Prefix matching the number (9E-9999):** no source found. **Unconfirmed.**

### Bonus: plates in daily life (game hook)
- From 7 Mar 2026, private cars could drive only on odd or even dates, by plate number. Fine 20,000 kyat. [OE] Reported abolished from 9 Sept 2026. [ON] A ready-made "plate rule" event for the game.

## 6. Game design takeaways

**Regex**
- Private / gov car: `^[1-9][A-HJ-NP-Z]-\d{4}$` (skip I and O for readability; real plates may use them).
- Taxi / bus: `^([A-H])\1-\d{4}$` (AA-HH, red).
- Top line: `^(YGN)-([1-9]|[1-3]\d|4[0-5])$` for Yangon townships 1-45.
- Bottom line: car make/model in caps.

**Colours** (see table in section 3)
- Private: black plate, white text. Government: same as private.
- Taxi / commercial / trailer: red, white text.
- Tourist: blue, white text.
- Diplomat / UN / IO: white, black text, `CD 12-34` / `UN 12-34` / `IO-1234`.
- Religious: yellow, black text. Hearse: reddish-brown.

**What drives price (ranked, with evidence)**
1. **All same digit (7777, 9999)** - 7-repeater phone number = 112m kyat, top of every auction. [NT] [IW]
2. **Lots of 9s** - 9999999 = 42m kyat, 2nd place; 9 "in high demand" for plates. [NT] [GT]
3. **Repeated 8s** - 8888888 = 33m kyat, 3rd. [NT]
4. **Digit sum = 9** (e.g. 7704, 4500) - real plate sold for 500,000 kyat. [GT]
5. **Contains 37 or 45/90** - cultural lucky numbers, no price found. [GT]
6. **Low number (0001-0999)** - choice fee higher for low numbers (amounts unconfirmed); wiki claim. [MO]
7. **Sequence (1234) / mirror (1221) / prefix match (9E-9999)** - no Myanmar evidence; use as small bonuses only. **Unconfirmed.**

Suggested scaling for the game: price multiplier from 1x (random) up to ~200x for "all 9s", keeping the real ratios: 7-repeat ≈ 2.7x the 9-repeat ≈ 3.4x the 8-repeat (Telenor 2016). Exact numbers are a design choice, not real data.
