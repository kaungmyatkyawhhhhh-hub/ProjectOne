# Roblox icon, thumbnail and logo

Read first (vault): `Roblox Playbook\Research notes\2026-11-05 PLAYBOOK - Roblox icon, thumbnail and logo recipe.md` (the full recipe + one-try prompt templates) and its three research notes (what the owner clicks, GFX logo style, 12 top games' thumbnails). Add every new lesson to the recipe note.

## What the owner clicks (his rules)
- ONE big hero, the player doing something (lunging, diving, biting at the camera), never standing still. Monster or transformation BEHIND him.
- The face is **bright white**, the brightest thing in the image, clean white key light, not tinted.
- Strong contrast, ONE colour scheme + its opposite; rare colours (pink, green, cyan) beat red.
- One glowing focal point; one huge number or "+1" stickers, or no text at all.
- The REAL classic Roblox avatar (Bacon Hair, smile decal, black jacket, blue t-shirt) in Roblox Blender GFX style; never a realistic human; nothing that looks AI-made, soft or slimy; objects that should be rigid look rigid; not too zoomed in.
- Original monsters only (no Venom or other known characters).
- **It must look like an EDITED thumbnail, not an in-game character** (owner 9 Nov: the 23x winner "actually looks like a thumbnail"; the loser "just looks like an in-game character with no edits"). Needs:
  - a story in the picture: transformation, attack or reaction, so it asks "what happens?";
  - GFX finishing: coloured rim light, the character lit to match the scene, depth (glow behind, dark falloff, DOF), painted details (cracks, glowing eyes, energy wrapping the hero);
  - one clear hero shape, with a background that supports him instead of competing.
  Fail test: if it could pass as a Studio screenshot with effects stickered on, redo the art direction, not just the colours.

## Modern style references (owner, 11 Nov: "match up to modern ui where everything is INSANE")
Vault: `Roblox Playbook/Research notes/MEGA BITE/2026-11-11 Research - Modern UI direction (owner feedback + all TikTok UIs)`. Read it before any icon, thumbnail or logo; it holds the owner's exact words and a breakdown of every TikTok he sent.
- **Reveal style (RNG Battlegrounds auras TikTok, @mojangopa):** a pitch-black backdrop, the subject lit dramatically by ONE hero colour, a clean bold white name label, and the game LOGO in polished chrome-metal letters with a bevel and a single gem. Use it for logos and for "dark" thumbnails (a monster or transformation reveal).
- **Bright style (the @.6ix13 AI-made shop TikTok):** saturated themed backgrounds with diagonal energy streaks, the hero object HUGE and breaking out of its frame, sparkles. Use it for bright, happy thumbnails.
- **Rejected by the owner:** the Shark Shop TikTok's UI and signage style (navy sign plate + marquee bulbs); only its MAP style is fine.
- Effects style (bbstudios / Claude-cutscene TikToks): one colour + darkness + emissive glow = premium; a black core + one hot colour + crescent slashes for energy.

## Watching TikTok references
Watch in the browser, never download: seek the `<video>` element, draw N frames onto a canvas contact sheet laid over the page, and screenshot it (zoom in on single frames for UI detail). When TikTok answers "Access Denied" in the owner's Chrome (a rate limit after ~11 videos), use the built-in browser (a separate profile) instead. Save each breakdown to the vault as you go.

## Workflow
1. Research: what the owner clicks + 10-20 live thumbnails/icons of the top games in the genre (thumbnails.roblox.com API in the browser). Save refs in the game's `Assets\...\refs\`.
2. Render the real avatar in Blender (Cycles, white key on the face, strong coloured rim lights, dark background, DOF).
3. ChatGPT makes the final art from a ONE-TRY prompt (templates in the recipe note): attach the render, ONE style reference, the logo for colours. Magenta/transparent rules as in the art pipeline. Attachments must come from the vault (commit the render into the vault, stage it, then upload); the outputs folder is rejected. Keep follow-ups (variants, the logo, a second thumbnail) in the SAME ChatGPT chat with "same exact style".
3b. **Two-pass finish (owner, 27 Nov 2026, GYM ARC icons: Gemini-only art "look very bad"):** ChatGPT first, to enhance the picture (composition, polish, GFX finishing). Then give ChatGPT's result to Gemini to make it NOT look AI-generated and enhance it once more. Never ship a single-pass Gemini or ChatGPT image as an icon.
3c. **Icons are not thumbnails (owner, 27 Nov 2026: two rounds rejected, "the reference image and the prompt is bad", study icons first).** Before any icon:
   - Pull 20 live icons of the genre + the top charts (`make_sheet.py`) and measure them. Copy what the top ones share, not what our thumbnails do. Study: `GYM ARC/Research/2026-11-27 GYM ARC - Icon study (gym icons + top CTR).md`.
   - One character, waist-up or closer, 70-90% of the frame, head big in the upper half, a face with eyes and an open expression (grin, shout, shock) that reads at 60 px. Hair over the eyes or a tiny decal mouth kills it.
   - Show the hook as ONE before -> after (small vs giant), not two equal full-body figures.
   - Bright, saturated, softly blurred background unless the genre is dark.
   - Seasonal icon = the same layout, props and background swapped, so returning players still recognise it.
   - **The reference is a LAYOUT MOCK**, not an old thumbnail: our real avatar cut out and placed at the exact sizes and positions, on the right background, with the logo zone left empty. The prompt names every position, size, expression and colour.
   - **Fill the frame (owner, 27 Nov 2026, v3 -> v4: "way better but other game icons still look better").** v3 had a full-body giant + a tiny kid: head 18% of the icon width vs 34-37% on the top gym icons, character saturation 87 vs 160-177, flat light, three things to read. v4 fixed it: waist-up, head ~35% of the width, double-biceps flex with the arms filling both edges, eyes to camera, hard warm key + coloured rim light, a saturated radial background (royal blue; festive red for Christmas), ONE hook prop (a height ruler on the edge, candy-cane striped for Christmas), no second character. Before showing the owner, build a one-row sheet of ours vs the 5 best genre icons and get an unanchored critique (a subagent measuring head width % and saturation) plus ChatGPT's; fix every gap first.
   - **Aesthetic build (owner, 27 Nov 2026, v4 -> v5: "our character's build look ugly because its not aesthetic", "study about bodybuilding aesthetics first").** Puffy balloon muscles and a boxy shirt read as ugly. Use classic proportions (study: `GYM ARC/Research/2026-11-27 GYM ARC - Bodybuilding aesthetics study (for character art).md`): shoulders about 3 head-widths, waist about half the shoulder width (drawn), hips about 0.6x shoulders, upper arm no bigger than the head, capped delts, flaring lats, crisp separations, fitted shirt. Attach a simple proportion-guide diagram (`v5/proportion_guide.py`) with the identity image, then measure the V-taper on the result and send one "keep EXACTLY, only fix X" follow-up. Crop mid-thigh so the waist shows; this beats the 35% head rule when the body is the selling point.
   - **Correction (owner, same day, on v5): "the arms and the waist is too small ... because of the obliques. it looks weird"; "powerlifters icon build is aesthetic"; "david laid at 16 was aesthetic".** Textbook ratios over-applied look wrong. Natural waist about 2/3 of the shoulders (about 1.5 : 1) with thick obliques and serratus, never a vacuumed 2 : 1 pinch; arms big (biceps about head-wide, thick forearms); dense, deeply separated 3D muscle. Use the owner's named examples (a top icon he likes) as the build reference over generic formulas, and ask which physiques he finds aesthetic before a body redesign.
   - **Second correction (owner, same day, during v6): "for roblox, the build should look robust".** Keep the V and natural waist, but the whole frame is sturdy and chunky to match the blocky head: thick neck, wide solid torso, heavy round shoulders, thick limbs, solid hips and thighs. Never lean or slim.
   - **Third correction (owner, same day, v6–v7):** he chose the aesthetic build over the robust one, with bigger legs (big quads, outer sweep); arms and legs must be the same material and colour. He called the Muscle Legends small-vs-giant before/after layout "sloppy, i want professionalism": for GYM ARC use ONE polished hero and show the hook through the scene (low camera, towering over the gym, a clean ruler), never a second small character with comic effects. When the owner says the icon "doesn't make sense", fix the hook in a professional way, not by copying a cheap genre layout.
   - **Fourth correction (owner, same day, v7):** "our one isnt focused on the character, its focused like 30% character, 30% logo, 30% background". The character IS the icon: waist-up, about 80% of the frame, head about 40% of the width, a small logo (about 28% wide), and a background that is only a soft blur. Gym League's focus is good, but it's too messy. Use one skin tone that harmonises with the palette (warm tan on blue), not gold limbs that clash with the face. A close crop must keep a long torso with a visible taper, or the character looks fat.
   - **Fifth correction (owner, same day, after v7e "looks bad"): "just try to have gym star's character physique, its aesthetic".** For GYM ARC, attach the Gym Star icon as a physique-only reference: smooth, rounded, toy-like muscle with soft simple shading and warm peach-tan skin; never hyper-detailed or veiny. Lesson: after two text-only body corrections fail, stop describing and attach the owner's named reference image.
   - **Sixth note (owner, same day, on v7i): "we need him to do some actions and make him the real focused zoomed in like how powerlifters does it (i actually think powerlifters icon is the best in there)".** A static flex is not enough: show the hero mid-action (a lift), zoomed in tight, with a big prop in the foreground for depth. Borrow only the framing from the reference; change the prop and colours so the icon does not look like a copy.
   - **Seventh note (owner, same day, on v8): "logo too small, they wont be able to read it. look at powerlifters gradients and colors and logo size".** Power Lifters logo: about 55% of the icon width. Two tones: the top word in a gradient matching the hero prop, the second word white, a thick dark outline, a warm neon border contrasting the background. GYM ARC: logo at 0.55 of the width, recoloured gold (`icon_render_25nov/recolor_logo_gold.py` makes `gymarc_logo_v11_gold.png`) to match the gold dumbbell and pop on blue. A logo matching the background colour disappears. Grazing the hair is fine.
   - **Eighth note (owner, same day): "the gradients are so ugly, all the options dont have a good color theme/palette".** A hue-shifted flat saturated background is not a palette. Measured recipe of the good icons (k-means): Power Lifters is 32% near-black plum base, saturated purple only as glow accents (~17%), plus warm orange skin; Gym Star is a dark teal base, cyan light, warm skin. So design palettes as: a DARK base of one hue with a vignette, the bright version of that hue only as light sources and rim light, a warm rim light plus warm skin as the contrast, and a white key light on the face. Render each palette in ChatGPT (background and lighting only), never with a code hue-shift. Logo: a designed vertical 2-stop gradient (light top to deep bottom) in the palette accent, a warm border, a dark plate in the base colour (`icon_render_25nov/recolor_logo_palette.py`).
   - **Ninth note (owner, same day): "i still dont like the gradient".** It was the chrome look: hard metallic bands and highlight stripes inside the letters. Power Lifters letters are clean, near-solid fills with only a soft top-to-bottom fade, the second word plain white, a black plate and one thick neon border glow. Use `icon_render_25nov/logo_clean_fill.py` (output `gymarc_logo_v13_<palette>.png`). Never chrome or striped text fills on GYM ARC logos.
   - **Tenth note (owner, same day): flat clean fills were "even worse"; he wants the shiny gradient of the Power Lifters 400 KG weight plate.** That plate is vivid glossy colour: light tint at the top, a soft bright sheen streak across the upper third (slightly diagonal), deep shade at the bottom, a neon edge glow and a pale edge line. It is NOT grey chrome with hard stripes and NOT a dark core. Code: `icon_render_25nov/logo_clean_fill.py`. Ask which "plate" the owner means before guessing.
   - **Eleventh note (owner, same day): he sent our ORIGINAL logo (gymarc_logo_v10_blue_simple.png) and said "like this gradient".** The original cyan-to-blue gradient logo was fine all along. It only looked weak because it sat on a flat bright blue background. Fix the background (dark palette) before redesigning a logo the owner already approved, and compare against the original before replacing it.
4. Claude adds the logo in code (never let ChatGPT draw it), keeps the original resolution, saves everything to the vault.
5. Small fixes: "keep EXACTLY the same, only change X"; otherwise fix locally (e.g. brighten the face by masking light low-saturation pixels).

## Tests before showing the owner (measure, don't guess)
- **Small-size test:** view the icon at 150, 100 and 60 px side by side with the current icon and 2-3 top games' icons. The hero and the hook must still read at 60 px.
- **Contrast numbers at 64 px:** about 10-15% near-white (lum > 215, low saturation) and 40-50% near-black (lum < 45). Busy bright art with < 30% dark fails: the face melts into the background. **These are for dark/reveal THUMBNAILS only. Icons: measure the genre first** (`measure.py` in `GYM ARC/Research/icon_study_2026-11-27/`). Gym icons, 27 Nov 2026: median luminance 0.51, near-black 11%; our dark 0.34 / 33% icons were rejected.
- **Blur test:** blur it; every key shape must still read.
- **Finishing / grade check (owner 9 Nov: same character render, but the losing icon's lighting, VFX, background, colours, contrast and exposure were all worse, and it got ~23x fewer clicks).** The render is not the icon; the grade is. Measured on his winner vs loser:
  - exposure: mean luminance about 0.28 (winner) vs 0.46 (loser, washed out and over-exposed);
  - blown highlights: < 20% clipped pixels (loser 25%);
  - colour: 1 colour family + its opposite, about 2 main hues (loser had 5 fighting hues). Strong saturation is fine inside one family;
  - face vs background: face about 20x+ brighter than the corners (winner 24x, loser 10x); corners near-black (lum < 0.05);
  - VFX sit around and behind the hero, never on top of the face, and never more than one glowing focal point.
  If a ChatGPT result fails these, fix the grade (darken/vignette the background, cut the hues, pull the exposure down, keep the face bright) before showing it.
- **Framing:** icon face ~70-80% of the width, everything 8% from the edges (rounded corners), no text/logo on the icon. Thumbnails: nothing important in the bottom ~15% (player counts), top-left free for the logo, test at 384x216.
- Judging two options: score both on these tests and say which wins and why, with the numbers.
